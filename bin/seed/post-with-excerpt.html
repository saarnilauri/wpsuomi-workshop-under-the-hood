A read ability and a write ability look almost identical when you register them. Both have a name, a description, a schema and two callbacks. The difference is what happens when the agent is wrong.

If a suggestion is wrong, a human reads it and discards it. If a write is wrong, the site changed and somebody has to notice. This is why the Abilities API carries behavioural annotations: readonly, destructive and idempotent. They are hints, not enforcement, but they are the hints a client uses to decide whether to ask you first.

The question worth arguing about over coffee is not whether agents should be allowed to write. It is who is accountable when they do, and whether your logs would let you reconstruct the decision a week later.
